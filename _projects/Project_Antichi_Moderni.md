*Star War: Antichi vs. Moderni* (*Star War: Ancients vs. Moderns*) is a board game I developed together with my students to illustrate, in a playful way, the origins of the Scientific Revolution. The game focuses in particular on the debates in astronomy and physics that, in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, pitted two camps against each other: on the one hand, the defenders of Ancient science (Aristotelian physics and Ptolemaic geocentric astronomy), and on the other, the proponents of Modern science (Galilean physics and Copernican heliocentric astronomy).

The two players take on the roles of the Ancients and the Moderns. Their goal is to explain as many celestial and terrestrial phenomena as possible, using the theories and philosophical resources that were historically available to the representatives of the two competing research programmes.

The game allows the players to explore a central moment in the history of science and to familiarize with key topics in the philosophy of science, such as the underdetermination of theories, the role of epistemic values, and scientific change.

## The game

*Star War* includes:

- 1 board
- 26 Phenomenon cards
- 29 Theory cards (13 for the Moderns, 16 for the Ancients)
- 18 Philosophy cards (9 for Moderns, 9 for the Ancients)
- 4 Epistemic Value cards
- 5 Telescopic Observation cards
- 20 Prediction pins
- 1 Censorship pin
