Teaching

Courses I currently teach

I currently teach three courses at the University of Turin: Filosofia della Scienza (Philosophy of Science) for the BA in Philosophy, Formal and Empirical Methods in Philosophy for the MA in Philosophy, and Scientometrics for the MA in Language Technologies and Digital Humanities.

Philosophy of Science

The course is an introduction to general philosophy of science and aims to present the main philosophical reflections on the nature, value and methods of science. It is organized in three main sections:

  • Static of Science: forms of scientific reasoning, empiricism and verification, confirmation and probability, falsificationism, underdetarmination, scientific explanation.
  • Dynamics of Science: theories of scientific change from Kuhn to Lakatos, realism vs. anti-realism
  • Science as a Social Process: the scientific community, science and society, science and values, the communication system of science

The full program and the syllabus are available on the course page.

Formal and Empirical Methods in Philosophy

The course offers a basic introduction to data science tailored on philosophy students, focusing in particular on data analysis and data visualization using R and RStudio.

It covers the following topics: The data cycle, data tables and tidy data, types of variables, introduction to R and RStudio (importing data in R, objects, functions), central tendency indicators (mean, median, mode) and order statistics (minimum, maximum, quartiles), selecting, filtering, and grouping data, the relational model: relational databases, cardinality of relationships, and join operations, data visualizations for univariate distributions of qualitative and quantitative variables (bar plot, box plot, histogram, Probability Mass Function and Cumulative Distribution Function), indicators of variability (range, variance, standard deviation), data visualizations for bivariate distributions of qualitative and quantitative variables (scatter plot), correlation, regression, deceiving data visualizations.

The full program and the syllabus are available on the course page.

Scientometrics

Scientometrics is the branch of applied statistics that studies scientific production and academic impact through suitable mathematical and quantitative methods. In the last decades, it has become central both in the investigation of scientific knowledge dynamics and in the science policy context, where scientometrics is often featured in the processes of evaluation of researchers and research institutions. The course aims to introduce students to this field, presenting both its theoretical-descriptive side (the so-called quantitative science studies) and its applied side (quantitative methods for research evaluation).

The course is structured in four main units. The first unit introduces students to the cycle of scientific research and the modern scientific communication system. The second presents the main indicators for assessing scientific production and impact. The third contextualizes evaluative scientometrics in the history of science policy, discusses the unintended effects of bibliometric evaluation, and presents the international debate on quantitative research assessment. The fourth unit focuses on quantitative science studies (descriptive scientometrics) and in particular on science mapping techniques: after an introduction to network analysis, specific bibliometric networks are presented and students learn to use VOSviewer, a free software especially designed for science mapping.

The full program and the syllabus are available on the course page.

Course 1: Data Science for Philosophy

Schedule: 6 hours per day with a lunch break on Friday for four weeks, plus one final presentation day.

Dates:

  • Friday 10 April
  • Friday 24 April
  • Friday 15 May
  • Friday 12 June
  • Friday 10 July (presentations day)

Short description: Over the past two decades, the range of methods used by philosophers to address philosophical problems has expanded significantly. Alongside traditional philosophical approaches, philosophers have increasingly experimented with methodologies drawn from the empirical and formal sciences, including experiments, simulations, data analysis, and formal modelling. Data plays a central role in all of these approaches. This course aims to provide philosophy students with solid foundations in data science, equipping them with the technical knowledge needed to manage, analyse, and extract insights from data. The course covers key concepts in statistics, data representation, data analysis, and data visualization, applying them to the investigation of real-world datasets related to philosophy and its history. Students will also become familiar with R, a standard programming language for computational statistics, acquiring practical skills that are valuable not only for academic research but also on the broader job market.

Course 2: Scientometrics and Science Mapping

Schedule: 6 hours per day with a lunch break on Saturday for four weeks, plus one final presentation day.

Dates:

  • Saturday 11 April
  • Saturday 25 April
  • Saturday 16 May
  • Saturday 13 June
  • Saturday 11 July (presentations day)

Short description: Scientometrics is the quantitative study of science. It provides a distinctive perspective on how scientific knowledge develops, spreads, and changes over time and, in the last decades, it has become central in the management of scientific research, especially in research evaluation. For philosophers, scientometrics offers empirical tools to investigate classic questions about scientific progress, disciplinary structure, collaboration, and the dynamics of research communities. This course aims to introduce students to scientometrics by presenting both its applied side (quantitative methods for research evaluation) and its descriptive side, with a particular focus on science mapping, i.e., a set of data-driven techniques for constructing maps of scientific fields based on large collections of scientific publications. Students will not only learn the theoretical foundations of science mapping, but also acquire hands-on experience in creating their own maps using VOSviewer, a freely available science mapping software.

Course 3: Quantitative Studies of Philosophy

Schedule and dates: From Monday 20 to Friday 24 July, 6 hours per day

Short description: Scholars in digital humanities, scientometrics, and philosophy itself are increasingly using quantitative methods to study the structure and dynamics of philosophy as a discipline. This course aims to provide an overview of the main methodologies used in quantitative studies of philosophy, presenting key research in this emerging field and discussing influential case studies. In addition, the course will host invited talks by leading scholars who apply quantitative approaches to areas such as the history of twentieth-century philosophy, integrated history and philosophy of science, and philosophy of science.
